The Prodigal Analyst

I trained myself out of the thing I enjoyed most. Then the tool everyone is scared of gave it back.

It starts at month end, with a variance I could not explain and the chase to find it. That chase was the job I loved. Cheap assembly just made it the whole job again.

Where the work went

Four things the tools cannot do for you, in Excel or anything else. Every essay here is about one of them.

01

Find the real question

"Why is opex up" is rarely the question. Usually it is a reclass, a timing difference, or one vendor. Working out which one is most of the job now.

02

Decide what is worth building

Any report can be built in minutes now. That is the trap. The judgment is in what you leave out of the package.

03

Direct the build

Tell the AI what to pull, then check its work the way you would check a new hire's workpaper. You cannot review what you do not understand.

04

Hand back something people trust

Say what the numbers support and what they do not. The note under the schedule is the part people remember.

What the tools handle now

Pulling the detail, joining it, tying it out, formatting the schedule, drawing the chart. Directed well, that is minutes, not days.

What still needs you

Knowing which variance matters, which one reverses next month, and what to say before the review meeting instead of after it.

The schedule builds itself now. Explaining it is still yours.

You can't direct what you don't understand.

AI did not remove the need to know the numbers. It raised the stakes and took away the hiding place. A fast, confident, wrong answer is still wrong, and someone still has to sign off on it.

I am packaging how I work into a method: finding the real question under the one I was handed, deciding what is worth building, directing the AI to do the work, and catching it when it is confidently wrong.
