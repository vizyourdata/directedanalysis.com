The thesis

Viz is the easy part. The analysis is the valuable part.

For twenty years an analyst's hours went to the mechanics. Cleaning, joining, reshaping, building, rebuilding. That was the job, because that was the hard part.

AI collapsed the assembly. Building the chart is now nearly free. The value did not vanish. It moved back to the part we always rushed past: framing the real question, deciding what is worth measuring, reading what the data actually says.

The thing you spend your time on is the thing that is actually valuable. So the question for every analyst is where the hours go now.

Issue 01 · the inaugural essay

The Valuable Part

The essay the whole publication grows out of. It opens on a confession, reframes the old job as assembly mistaken for analysis, names the automation fear honestly, and then turns it.

I am not a coder.

Think about where your hours actually went. Not where the job description said they went. Where they went. For most of us it was the assembly: the joins, the reshaping, the chart that had to be rebuilt after every meeting. We called that analysis because it was hard and it was ours.

The machine came for the assembly. It did not come for the thinking. It raised the stakes on the thinking and removed the hiding place.

I started in Excel, making homely line graphs that make me cringe now. Fifteen-plus years and thousands of dashboards later, I still think the cringe is the credibility. I have made most of the expensive mistakes already.
