For fifteen years my hours went to assembly. Pulling the detail, tying it out, rebuilding the schedule. I started in Excel making line graphs I still cringe at.

Then the tools came and the assembly got cheap. What it gave back is the part that always got squeezed: finding out why the number moved, deciding what matters, checking the answer, handing back something people can act on.

I have done that part for fifteen years, in whatever hours the assembly left over. It was always the valuable part. Now it gets the week.

That part is what this cohort teaches.

The method

How I turn a vague question into a solved problem.

It is not a course about a tool. Tools change. This is how I think.

How I find the real question under the one I was handed. How I decide what is worth building and what to throw out. How I direct the AI to do the work, and how I catch it when it is confidently wrong. How I hand back something the business can actually trust.

Six weeks, one hard case

I hand you the case on day one: a business, its data, and a request from someone who does not quite know what they are asking for. It is built from real projects, so it has the problems real projects have. Each week you take it one step further.

Every step also comes with a conversation: with the person who asked, the person who owns the data, the person who booked the entry. We practice that conversation in pairs each week, before you have it for real.

WEEK 01

Find the real question

Rewrite the request the way an analyst would: what is measured, against what, at what level of detail, over what period, and why it matters. Then confirm it with the person who asked.

WEEK 02

Decide what is worth building

Size the problem before you solve it. Is the gap big enough to matter? Compared to what? Write down what you will not build.

WEEK 03

Direct the build

Hand the assembly to AI with a brief it cannot misread. You decide. It types.

WEEK 04

Catch it when it is wrong

Tie every number back to its source. Sort every cause as real spend, timing, or a booking error. Nothing goes in the write up without a query that proves it.

WEEK 05

Hand back something people trust

The findings, the so what, and a note a CFO would sign off on, all drawn from the workpaper.

WEEK 06

Demo day

You walk the group through your work on the case, from the request you were handed to the question you answered.

Questions

Do I need to code?

No. I am not a coder either. You will direct the work, not write it.

Which tools?

Whatever you use now. Excel, SQL, Tableau, Power BI, all fine. The method does not care.

Which AI?

Whatever your company allows. If it allows nothing yet, you will still learn the method, and you will be ready when it does.

Do I bring my own project?

No. I give you the case, so I can make it as hard as it needs to be. The method carries straight back to your own work.

What if I miss a session?

Tell the group ahead of time if you can, catch up from the group's notes, and bring your questions to the next session. Some sessions are recorded, but not all of them, so please do not plan on watching later.

Why ask for every week?

Because the group is half of what you are paying for. Everyone is there to give feedback on each other's work and to practice the conversations together. A seat that sits empty is feedback someone else does not get, and a partner they do not have.

Will there be another cohort?

The plan is to run more. I will not promise when, or at what price.
